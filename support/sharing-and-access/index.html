Share content and control access

Choose learner sharing links, configure expiry and access limits, and understand guest access and link regeneration.

Wistudi Support · Updated 18 November 2026
Quick answer

Use Share or Share with Learners on published content. Choose the available learner link, review its access settings and copy it after the settings have saved.

Share the learner experience

Open the published Flow or XP Video in your workspace and choose Share or Share with Learners. Use the resulting learner link, not the builder URL or a creator preview URL.

A preview is intended for checking content. Learners should use the invitation or access link designed for participation so they receive the correct access instructions.

Before sharing, decide who needs access, whether the link should stop accepting new learners after a date, and whether an access limit is appropriate.

Choose an available link type

The sharing panel depends on the content and its publication state. A public link can be unavailable when the content is not on Discover; that does not necessarily mean the workspace version failed to publish.

  • Public Link: use it when the panel makes it available and you intend the linked content to be accessible to that audience.
  • Temporary Link: on a Flow, generate a controlled link and review the expiry and access settings shown.
  • Private Access Link: on interfaces that offer it, use the protected link and its access controls.

If public sharing is unavailable, use the permitted controlled-link option rather than turning on a public listing just to bypass the access setting. Discover visibility and learner link management are different decisions.

Generate, save and copy a link

  1. Open the sharing panel on the intended published content or version.
  2. Review the available link and its active, inactive or expired status.
  3. Set an Expiry Date and Access Limit where offered. Read whether settings apply immediately or when the link is generated.
  4. Choose Generate Link if a link does not yet exist. Use the displayed save action when settings require saving.
  5. Wait for the saved or generated confirmation, then choose Copy.
  6. Test the copied learner link before sending it to your audience.

An access limit controls new use of that link. If the limit has been reached, review the setting or ask for help rather than repeatedly sending the same unavailable invitation.

Where share-to-service buttons are offered, check which URL they will use. A Flow can use its Temporary Link when a public link is unavailable. The destination service may have its own account and posting requirements.

Understand regeneration and expiry

Regenerate is not the same as copying the current link. The current sharing interface warns that regeneration can make the old link inactive. For Flow temporary links, the confirmation explains that the current link stops working for people who have not opened it yet, while learners who already joined keep their access.

Read that confirmation before proceeding. If you issue a replacement link, update the invitation or message that your audience will use.

Expiry or deactivation can prevent future access through a link. Do not assume it deletes earlier submissions, removes every learner relationship or reverses an assignment. Check the settings and contact support if you need to change access for people who already joined.

Understand guest and signed-in access

The invitation can offer Continue as Guest, require sign-in or restrict access according to the content's settings. Do not promise guest access unless it is offered on the actual link.

Guest progress may be limited to the browser session, and recorded learning features may be unavailable. When you need an identifiable learner record or assessment history, instruct the learner to sign in with the intended account before starting.

Tell recipients to use the complete link and return to it after sign-in if necessary. See Find and access learning for the learner-facing instructions.

Check before sending to a group

Confirm the lesson title, version and access settings. Open the learner link in a separate signed-out browser session to see the invitation state, without completing an attempt that could use a limited access slot.

If the link reports unavailable, expired or an access limit, check the publisher's settings first. Send private links only to the intended recipients. A link that works for you as the owner is not proof that every recipient has permission.
