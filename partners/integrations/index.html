Partner integration overview

Integrating with Wistudi

A practical reference for product, partnership and engineering teams evaluating how an external learning tool, content platform or LMS can connect with Wistudi.

Updated September 2026  •  Product and technical overview
Early partner applications are open for the 15 October 2026 integration launch.

Wistudi is speaking with selected EdTech platforms, publishers and specialist learning tools about early integrations and pilot use cases.

Overview

Wistudi is a learning publishing and delivery platform built around Flows. A Flow is a complete learning experience that can combine explanation, media, interactive activities, assessment, partner content and supporting resources in one sequence.

Flows are made from Blocks. A Block is a focused unit inside the Flow. It can contain a Wistudi activity or resource, or, where an integration supports it, an external partner experience.

For partner integrations, Wistudi is designed around a simple principle: the learner should remain in the learning context wherever possible. When the partner product allows it, the external experience should open directly inside the Flow, use a low-friction launch, and return completion or score information when that information is useful.

Preferred experience: partner content appears as an inline Block in the Flow. Wistudi handles the surrounding lesson and learning sequence, while the partner provides the specialist experience inside that Block.

Two integration directions

Before discussing protocols, it is important to separate two different integration directions. They solve different problems and use different mechanisms.

DirectionTypical useHow results move
Partner inside WistudiA partner activity, content item or application appears inside a Wistudi Flow as a Block.The partner can return completion or score information to Wistudi when Connected Access & Results is used.
Wistudi inside another platformAn LMS or learning platform launches a specific Wistudi Flow or XP Video from its own course or interface.Where applicable, Wistudi can return a grade to the external platform through LTI Assignment and Grade Services.

These directions should not be treated as the same integration. A partner can support one direction without supporting the other.

Key Wistudi terms

Flow
The complete learning experience. A Flow can be a focused lesson or a larger sequence containing content, interaction, assessment and partner experiences.
Block
A focused unit inside a Flow, such as text, video, an activity, assessment, resource or partner application.
Inline integration
A partner experience that opens and runs inside the Flow rather than sending the learner to a separate destination.
Creator
The teacher, publisher, trainer or other authorised user building the Flow and selecting the learning experiences it contains.
Interactive + printable
Wistudi supports digital learning and printable output in the same publishing workflow. A partner activity can remain digital while the wider Flow can also support worksheets and printable resources.

Partners Wistudi is looking to work with

We are especially interested in specialist products that become more useful when they can be placed intentionally inside a broader lesson, curriculum or learning sequence.

  • Interactive learning tools, including simulations, manipulatives, creative tools and practice environments.
  • Assessment and practice platforms, including quizzes, diagnostics, adaptive practice and specialist assessment.
  • Content providers and publishers, including reading platforms, curriculum providers, educational media and digital libraries.
  • Subject-specific EdTech for mathematics, STEM, literacy, languages, coding and other specialist areas.
  • AI learning applications with a clear educational use case and a learner or teacher-guided experience.
  • LMS and learning platforms that want to launch Wistudi content or exchange results through an established interoperability model.

Integration options at a glance

Partners do not need to implement every level. Most integrations should start with the simplest model that solves the use case and add capability only when it creates a clear improvement.

LevelIntegrationPurpose
1Simple EmbedShow a self-contained partner page or activity directly inside a Flow.
2Connected Access & ResultsRecognise the learner through a trusted launch and return completion or score information to Wistudi.
3Content Workflow IntegrationLet creators select, create or edit partner content without copying URLs between systems.
4Extended Data SharingAdd only the specific learner, class, group or role information a partner experience genuinely needs.
5Advanced, LTI 1.3 or CustomSupport reverse-direction launches or a custom API/webhook integration when the standard Wistudi launch model is not the right fit.

Level 1: Simple Embed

Simple Embed is the minimum integration. The partner provides a content URL and Wistudi loads that page in an embedded view when the learner reaches the relevant Block.

There is no Wistudi security handshake, connected identity, callback, completion event or score return at this level. The page is expected to function as a self-contained experience.

Partner requirements

  • Provide a stable HTTPS content URL.
  • Allow the page to be displayed in an embedded view.
  • Avoid frame or content-security restrictions that prevent the Wistudi origin from loading the page.
  • Ensure the activity works correctly at the dimensions available inside a Flow and on supported learner devices.
Use Simple Embed when: the partner experience is useful inside the lesson but does not need Wistudi to identify the learner or record a result.

Level 2: Connected Access and Results

Connected Access is used when the learner should enter the partner experience without a second login and Wistudi needs to know when the activity has been completed or what score it produced.

Launch from Wistudi to the partner

Before loading the partner content, Wistudi generates a signed launch token and appends it to the launch URL. The current model uses HMAC signing with a secret shared during partner setup.

The launch contains a pseudonymous learner identifier, Flow reference, step reference and a short expiry. The current default expiry is five minutes. The partner reads the token, verifies the signature, checks the expiry, and rejects invalid or expired launches.

Launch data pseudonymous studentId + flowId + stepId + exp

No learner name, email address or other personal information is included in the standard connected launch.

Completion and score returned to Wistudi

When the learner finishes, the embedded page sends a browser message back to the Wistudi window that opened it. Wistudi validates the message origin against the partner domain registered during setup before accepting the event.

Scored activity event: complete + score + maxScore

Completion-only activity completed: true

A score is not required for every activity. If the activity is ungraded, completion alone is enough. If it is graded, the partner should return both the achieved score and maximum score so Wistudi can interpret the result consistently.

Shared setup for Level 2

  • Partner launch URL.
  • Registered partner domain used for origin validation.
  • Shared signing secret.
  • Agreement on whether the partner returns completion only or a numeric score.

Grading and result handling

There are two distinct grading directions in Wistudi integrations. This distinction is important because they use different mechanisms.

ScenarioWho sends the result?How it is returned
Partner activity inside WistudiThe partner sends completion or score information to Wistudi.Level 2 completion event from the embedded partner page. A scored activity returns score and maxScore.
Wistudi content inside another LMSWistudi sends the applicable grade to the external platform.LTI 1.3 Assignment and Grade Services (AGS), where grading is part of the agreed integration.

These mechanisms should not be confused. AGS is used for standards-based grade return when Wistudi is launched as an LTI Tool. It is not the mechanism described for a normal Level 2 partner activity embedded inside a Wistudi Flow.

Result patterns

  • No tracked result: appropriate for a Level 1 content experience.
  • Completion only: Wistudi records that the learner finished the partner activity.
  • Numeric score: the partner returns score and maximum score to Wistudi.
  • Grade returned to an LMS: Wistudi uses AGS when Wistudi itself is the LTI Tool and grade return is part of the integration.

Level 3: Content Workflow Integration

Content Workflow Integration is designed for partners whose activities should be selected or created from inside the Wistudi creator experience rather than added by pasting a URL.

Content picker

The partner hosts a selection screen, or launcher, that Wistudi opens using the same signed-launch approach as Connected Access. The creator selects an existing activity and the launcher returns the activity reference to Wistudi through an agreed browser message or redirect callback.

Create or edit in place

The same pattern can open the partner's creation or editing screen. After saving, the partner returns the new or updated activity reference so Wistudi can attach it to the relevant Block.

Important relationship to learner launch

Level 3 controls how a creator chooses or creates the content. It does not replace the learner runtime integration. If the selected activity also needs connected learner access or result return, the learner-facing experience should use the Connected Access & Results model as well.

Partner requirements

  • Host the selection and/or creation interface.
  • Accept the trusted creator launch agreed with Wistudi.
  • Return a stable activity reference that Wistudi can store.
  • Confirm the return method, either browser message or redirect callback, during implementation.
  • Define whether existing content can be edited after it has been attached to a Flow.

Level 4: Extended Data Sharing

Extended Data Sharing is an optional add-on to a connected integration. It is not a required step after Levels 2 or 3.

If the partner activity genuinely needs additional context, Wistudi can add specific fields to the Level 2 signed launch. Examples may include a role or group identifier. Additional fields are agreed case by case rather than included by default.

Data-minimisation rule: request only the information the partner experience needs to function. The standard connected launch remains pseudonymous and does not include a learner name or email address.

Level 5: Advanced and custom integrations

Advanced integrations cover partner systems that do not fit the normal launch-token model and the reverse direction, where Wistudi content is launched from another LMS or learning platform.

Custom API or webhook connection

Where a partner exposes a different integration model, Wistudi can scope a dedicated API or webhook contract. Because these integrations depend on the partner's product and available interfaces, the data contract, authentication method, events and responsibilities are defined during technical scoping rather than assumed in this overview.

Wistudi inside another platform: LTI 1.3

When an LMS or learning platform wants to launch Wistudi content from inside its own course environment, the intended standards-based direction is LTI 1.3. In this model, the external system is the LTI Platform and Wistudi is the LTI Tool.

Tool registration

The platform registers Wistudi as an LTI 1.3 Tool using the normal registration information for the deployment. This includes a client ID, deployment ID, Wistudi login and redirect URLs, and the Wistudi public JWKS URL.

OIDC launch and JWKS validation

Launches use the standard OpenID Connect third-party login flow. Signed messages are verified using the public keys exposed through JWKS rather than the shared-secret approach used by the Wistudi Level 2 integration.

Deep Linking

Deep Linking allows authorised staff in the external platform to choose specific Wistudi content, such as a Flow or XP Video, and place that content into a course or assignment rather than linking only to Wistudi generally.

Assignment and Grade Services (AGS)

Where the Wistudi experience is graded and grade return is enabled for the integration, AGS allows Wistudi to send the resulting grade back to the external platform's gradebook.

Roster and role information

Roster information is not part of the base integration described in the Wistudi partner guide. If a partner use case requires roster, course membership or additional role data, that requirement should be identified during scoping and agreed separately rather than assumed.

Partner readiness checklist

Before implementation, the partner and Wistudi teams should be able to answer the following questions clearly.

  • What learner or creator problem is the integration solving?
  • Is the experience being launched inside Wistudi, or is Wistudi being launched inside the partner platform?
  • Can the learner experience run inside an iframe or other embedded view?
  • If not, what launch behaviour is required?
  • Does the learner need a separate account or login?
  • Does the activity produce no result, completion only, or a numeric score?
  • Which system is responsible for grading?
  • Which system needs to receive the grade or completion record?
  • Does the creator need to browse, create or edit partner content from Wistudi?
  • What learner, class, group or role information is strictly required?
  • Are there iframe, CSP, cookie or browser restrictions that affect the learner experience?
  • If LTI is involved, which LTI 1.3 capabilities are already supported by the partner platform?
  • What sandbox, test content or non-production environment is available?
  • Who will be the technical contact on each side during implementation and testing?

Recommended integration testing

The exact test plan depends on the integration, but the following checks cover the essential Wistudi behaviours.

  • Embed: activity loads correctly inside the Flow and remains usable on expected learner devices.
  • Connected launch: valid tokens are accepted; invalid and expired tokens are rejected.
  • Login: learner is not unexpectedly asked to authenticate again when connected access is intended.
  • Completion: an ungraded activity can return completion without a score.
  • Scoring: a graded activity returns both score and maximum score correctly.
  • Origin validation: result messages from an unregistered origin are not accepted.
  • Content picker: selected or created content returns a stable reference that can be reopened from the Flow.
  • Deep Linking: the external platform can select the intended Wistudi content when LTI Deep Linking is in scope.
  • Grade return: Wistudi grade information reaches the external gradebook correctly when AGS is in scope.

Partner integration process

  1. Use-case review. Define what the partner product contributes to the Flow and the intended learner and creator experience.
  2. Choose the integration direction and model. Confirm whether this is partner content inside Wistudi, Wistudi inside another platform, or both.
  3. Define data and result flow. Agree what Wistudi sends, what the partner sends back, whether grading applies, and which system owns the final record.
  4. Technical setup. Exchange the required URLs, domains, signing details, LTI registration information or custom contract.
  5. Sandbox or pilot testing. Test authentication, embedding, completion, scoring, content selection and grade return as applicable.
  6. Experience review. Confirm that the integration works as part of a real Flow and does not introduce unnecessary login or navigation friction.
