Partner integration overview

Integrating with Wistudi

This article explains the main ways an external learning tool, content provider or LMS can integrate with Wistudi, what each integration level is intended to support, and what partner teams should consider before technical scoping begins.

Audience: product, partnership and engineering teams  •  Updated September 2026
Early partner applications are open for the 15 October 2026 launch.

Wistudi is currently preparing its first partner integrations and is speaking with selected EdTech platforms, publishers and specialist learning tools.

Overview

Wistudi is a learning publishing and delivery platform built around Flows. A Flow is a complete learning experience that can combine explanation, media, interactive activities, assessment, partner content and supporting resources in one sequence.

Flows are made from Blocks. A Block is a focused unit inside the Flow. It can contain Wistudi content or activity types, or, where an integration supports it, an external partner experience.

Our integration model is designed around one central principle: when a partner tool is part of a lesson, the learner should remain in context wherever possible. We therefore prefer integrations that can open and work directly inside the Flow rather than repeatedly sending the learner to a separate product and back again.

Preferred learner experience: partner content appears as an inline Block in the Flow, learner access is handled with as little friction as possible, and completion or score information can return to Wistudi when the partner product supports it.

Key Wistudi terms

Flow
The complete learning experience. A Flow may be a lesson, activity sequence, larger program or curriculum experience.
Block
A focused unit inside a Flow, such as content, video, an activity, assessment, external application or partner experience.
Inline integration
A partner experience that opens and remains inside the Flow rather than launching the learner into a separate destination.
Interactive + printable
Wistudi supports digital learning and printable output in the same publishing workflow. A partner experience may remain digital while the surrounding Flow can also support worksheets and printable resources.

Partners Wistudi is looking to work with

We are particularly interested in products that add specialist capability to a broader learning experience and are more useful when they can be placed intentionally inside a lesson or curriculum.

  • Interactive learning tools, including simulations, manipulatives, creative tools and practice environments.
  • Assessment and practice platforms, including quizzes, diagnostics, adaptive practice and specialist assessment.
  • Content providers and publishers, including reading platforms, curriculum providers, educational media and digital libraries.
  • Subject-specific EdTech for areas such as mathematics, STEM, literacy, languages and coding.
  • AI learning applications with a clear educational purpose and a useful teacher-guided or learner-facing experience.
  • LMS and learning platforms that want to launch Wistudi content, exchange results or explore a two-way workflow.

Integration models

Wistudi supports several integration levels. Partners do not need to implement every level. In most cases, the right approach is to begin with the smallest integration that solves the use case and add capability later if it is useful.

LevelIntegrationPurposeStatus
1Simple EmbedShow a partner activity or page directly inside a Flow with no connected login or result capture.In development
2Connected Access & ResultsRecognise the learner through a trusted launch and return completion or score information to Wistudi.In development
3Content Workflow IntegrationLet creators select, create or edit partner content without copying links between systems.Planned
4Extended Data SharingAdd specific learner, class, group or role information when the partner experience genuinely requires it.Planned as needed
5Advanced / LTI / CustomSupport reverse-direction launches, LTI 1.3 or custom API and webhook integrations for defined partner requirements.Scoped per partner

Level 1: Simple Embed

Simple Embed is intended for a partner activity or content page that can already run securely inside an embedded browser view. The partner provides a content URL and Wistudi loads that URL inside the Flow when the learner reaches the relevant Block.

There is no login handshake, identity transfer, callback or result capture at this level. The partner experience is expected to work as a self-contained page.

Typical partner requirement: the page must permit embedding and must not block Wistudi through frame restrictions or incompatible content-security settings.

Level 2: Connected Access and Results

Connected Access adds a trusted launch so the partner can recognise the learner without asking for a separate login. Wistudi generates a signed, short-lived launch token containing a pseudonymous learner identifier together with Flow and step references.

The partner validates the signature and expiry before granting access. Wistudi does not need to send a learner name or email address for the normal connected launch.

When the learner completes the activity, the partner can return a completion event and, where applicable, a score. Wistudi validates the origin of the returned message before accepting it.

Typical Level 2 exchange Launch: pseudonymous learner ID + Flow reference + Block/step reference + short expiry
Return: completion only, or score + maximum score when the activity is graded

Level 3: Content Workflow Integration

Content Workflow Integration is designed for partners whose content should be selected or created from inside Wistudi rather than added by pasting a URL.

The intended model is a partner-hosted launcher. Wistudi opens the launcher using a trusted launch. The creator selects an existing activity or creates/edits one, and the partner returns the resulting content reference to Wistudi so it can be saved against the relevant Block.

This level is especially relevant to publishers, assessment platforms and specialist EdTech applications with large content libraries or authoring tools.

Level 4: Extended Data Sharing

Some partner experiences need more than a pseudonymous learner ID. Examples may include a role, group reference or class reference. These fields are not included by default.

Additional data is scoped case by case and should be limited to the minimum information required for the partner experience to function.

Level 5: LTI 1.3 and custom integrations

Advanced integration covers cases that do not fit the standard inline launch model and the reverse direction, where another LMS or platform wants to launch Wistudi content.

For reverse-direction LMS integrations, Wistudi's planned standard is LTI 1.3. A platform registers Wistudi as an LTI 1.3 tool and launches Wistudi content using the normal LTI security and launch flow.

LTI 1.3 overview

LTI 1.3 is relevant when Wistudi content is launched from another LMS or learning platform, and in partner cases where standard LTI interoperability is the appropriate connection model.

Wistudi as an LTI Tool

When another platform launches Wistudi, that platform acts as the LTI Platform and Wistudi acts as the LTI Tool. Registration is expected to include the client ID, deployment ID, Wistudi login and redirect URLs, and the Wistudi public JWKS endpoint.

OIDC launch and JWKS

LTI 1.3 launches use the standard OpenID Connect third-party login flow. Signed launch messages are verified using public keys published through JWKS. This replaces the shared-secret model associated with older LTI versions.

Deep Linking

Deep Linking allows a creator or administrator in the external platform to select a specific Wistudi learning experience, such as a Flow or XP Video, rather than linking only to Wistudi generally.

Assignment and Grade Services (AGS)

Where the launched Wistudi experience is graded, AGS can be used to report a grade back to the external platform's gradebook. The exact score and completion behaviour is confirmed during the integration scope.

Names and Roles Provisioning Services (NRPS)

Roster or role information is not required for every integration. Where a partner use case depends on course membership, roles or roster data, NRPS or another agreed data mechanism can be considered as part of the scope.

Learner experience and launch behaviour

For partner content inside Wistudi, our preferred experience is inline. The partner activity should remain inside the Flow so the learner retains the lesson context before and after the partner interaction.

A separate tab or window may still be appropriate when the partner product requires it for authentication, security or technical reasons. The required launch behaviour should therefore be confirmed during scoping rather than assumed from LTI support alone.

Completion, scores and grading

Not every partner activity needs to return a grade. Wistudi integrations can support different result patterns depending on what the activity actually produces.

  • No result: suitable for content or tools that do not need completion tracking.
  • Completion only: suitable when Wistudi only needs to know that the learner finished the partner activity.
  • Score: suitable when the partner activity produces a numeric result that should be captured by Wistudi.
  • LTI grade return: applicable when Wistudi content is launched in another platform and a grade needs to be passed back through AGS.

Security and data handling

The integration level determines the security mechanism. Simple Embed does not include a launch handshake. Connected Access uses a signed launch token and domain/origin validation. LTI 1.3 uses OIDC and JWKS-based signing.

Wistudi's default approach is to minimise personal data exchanged with partner systems. A pseudonymous learner identifier is sufficient for the normal connected launch. Additional learner, class or role data is considered only where the partner can explain why it is required.

Information we ask partners to confirm

Before technical work begins, Wistudi normally needs the partner team to confirm the points below.

  • Can the learner experience be embedded in an iframe or another inline view?
  • If not, what launch behaviour is required?
  • Does the learner need a separate account or login?
  • Does the activity produce completion, a score or another result?
  • Can the partner return that result to Wistudi?
  • Does the creator need to browse or create partner content from inside Wistudi?
  • What learner, role, class or group data is actually required?
  • Does the partner currently support LTI 1.3, Deep Linking, AGS or NRPS?
  • Are there iframe, CSP, cookie or browser restrictions that affect embedded use?
  • What test or sandbox environment is available for integration work?

Partner integration process

  1. Product and use-case review. We establish what the partner product contributes to the Flow and what the learner and creator experience should be.
  2. Integration model selection. We determine whether Simple Embed, Connected Access, Content Workflow, LTI 1.3 or a custom connection is the right fit.
  3. Technical scoping. Both engineering teams confirm endpoints, launch behaviour, security, data fields, result handling and test requirements.
  4. Sandbox or pilot test. The integration is tested with a limited use case before wider rollout.
  5. Experience review. We review not only whether the integration works technically, but whether it behaves well as part of a real Wistudi Flow.
