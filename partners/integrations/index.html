Partner integration guide

How partner integrations work in Wistudi

A practical overview for product, partnership and engineering teams exploring how an external learning tool, content platform or LMS can connect with Wistudi.

Audience: Product, partnership and engineering teams Updated September 2026 Approx. 8 minute read
Early partner applications are open ahead of our 15 October 2026 integration launch.

We are currently speaking with selected EdTech, content and learning-platform teams about first integrations and pilot opportunities.

Apply as an early partner

Wistudi is designed around complete learning experiences rather than isolated activities. That shapes how we think about integrations. The goal is not simply to place another product link inside Wistudi. Where the partner technology allows it, we want that experience to become part of the lesson itself.

In practice, that means a partner tool may appear directly inside a Wistudi Flow, sit alongside Wistudi activities and content, and return completion or results without forcing the learner through unnecessary logins or navigation steps.

The question we ask first is not “Can these two systems connect?” It is “Can this partner experience become a coherent part of the learning Flow?”

Starting context: Flow, Blocks and printable learning

A Flow is the complete learning experience in Wistudi. It can be short, such as a focused lesson, or substantial enough to represent a broader course, curriculum or learning sequence.

Flows are built from Blocks. A Block is a focused piece of the experience, such as explanatory content, an interactive activity, a video, an assessment, an external resource or a partner application.

For integrations, the Block is important because it is the point where an external product can become part of the wider lesson rather than sit beside it as a separate destination.

FlowThe complete learning experience that brings content, interaction, assessment, media and partner tools together in sequence.
BlockA focused unit inside a Flow. Partner experiences are designed to appear as Blocks where the technical model supports it.
Interactive + printableWistudi connects digital learning with printable output. A partner experience may remain digital while the wider Flow can also produce or reference printable learning material.

Why inline matters

A learner should not feel that the lesson stopped because another application opened. That is why our preferred integrations keep the partner experience inside the Flow wherever technically possible.

Not every partner needs all three stages. A useful integration can begin with a simple embedded experience and add connected access or result handling later.

There are cases where a partner needs to launch in a new tab because of browser security, authentication or product architecture. We can work with that. It is simply not our preferred learner experience when a stronger inline option is available.

The same principle applies to access and results. If a learner is already in Wistudi, we prefer not to ask for another login unless it is genuinely necessary. If an activity produces a meaningful completion state or score, we prefer that result to become available to the wider Wistudi learning experience where the partner supports it.

Integration options

Partners do not need to implement every level. In many cases the right approach is to start with the smallest integration that creates real value and extend it only when there is a reason to do so.

Level 1: Simple Embed

This is the fastest path when a partner already has a page or activity that can be shown securely inside an iframe. Wistudi loads that experience directly in the lesson. There is no security handshake, no learner identity transfer and no result capture at this level.

Level 2: Connected Access and Results

This level adds trusted access and basic result handling. Wistudi creates a signed, short-lived launch token containing a pseudonymous learner identifier and the relevant Flow and step references. The partner verifies the token before granting access.

When the activity is finished, the partner can return completion or score information to Wistudi. The objective is a low-friction learner experience without requiring names, email addresses or other personal information by default.

Level 3: Content Workflow Integration

This is intended for partners where creators need to select or create content rather than simply paste a link. The partner hosts a selection or editing screen, Wistudi launches it, and the chosen activity reference is returned to the relevant Block.

The benefit is practical: the teacher or publisher does not need to move back and forth between two systems copying URLs.

Level 4: Extended Data Sharing

Some partner products genuinely need more context, such as role, class or group membership. In those cases we can consider additional fields, but only the specific information needed for the experience to work.

Level 5: LTI 1.3 and custom integrations

This covers more advanced cases, including the reverse direction where another LMS or learning platform wants to launch Wistudi content.

LTI 1.3 direction

  • Launch: standard LTI 1.3 with OIDC and JWKS-based signing.
  • Content selection: Deep Linking for selecting a specific Wistudi Flow or XP Video.
  • Grade return: Assignment and Grade Services where a Wistudi activity needs to report a grade back.
  • Custom cases: dedicated APIs or webhooks can be scoped when the standard launch model does not fit the partner product.

What we look at before scoping an integration

A more complicated integration is not automatically a better one. We start with the learner experience and the practical value created for both products.

Can the experience work inside a Flow?We look first at whether it can be embedded or launched while preserving lesson context.
Does the learner need another login?If so, we assess whether a connected launch, LTI or another trusted method can remove that friction.
What should come back to Wistudi?For some products, completion is enough. Others may need a score or another agreed result.
How should a creator add the content?We establish whether a normal URL is enough or whether a content picker, Deep Linking or creation workflow would materially improve the experience.
What data is genuinely required?We prefer pseudonymous access and only add learner or class information that the partner product actually needs.

Common questions

Do we need to support LTI 1.3 to become a Wistudi partner?

No. LTI 1.3 is one advanced pathway, especially when another LMS or learning platform wants to launch Wistudi content. A partner experience inside Wistudi may begin with a simple embed or connected launch instead.

Does our product have to run inside an iframe?

No, but inline delivery is our preferred learner experience. If security or product architecture requires a separate launch, we can assess that during scoping.

Do we have to return grades?

No. Some partner experiences are useful without grading. An activity may return completion only, a score where one exists, or no result at the simplest integration level.

Can teachers select partner content directly from Wistudi?

That is the planned direction for Content Workflow Integration. The intended model is a partner-hosted selection or creation screen that returns the selected activity reference to Wistudi.

Can Wistudi content launch inside our LMS?

That is part of the advanced reverse-direction integration scope. The planned model uses standard LTI 1.3, including Deep Linking and grade return where required.
